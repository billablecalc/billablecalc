Late Fee Calculator for Unpaid Invoices

See how much late interest to add to an overdue invoice, with a monthly or yearly rate and an optional flat fee.

How to calculate a late fee

Late interest = unpaid amount × monthly rate × days past due / 30. With a yearly rate, divide by 365 instead. Example: $1,000 at 1.5% a month, 45 days late = $22.50, so the client now owes $1,022.50.

The rate you can charge depends on your contract and your state. If your contract names a late fee, that is your starting point. If it does not, you can usually claim only the state’s legal interest rate.

Questions

How do I calculate a late fee on an invoice?

Multiply the unpaid amount by your monthly rate and by the days past due divided by 30. For example, $1,000 at 1.5% a month, 45 days late: 1,000 × 1.5% × 45 / 30 = $22.50.

What is a normal late fee for an invoice?

Many businesses use 1% to 1.5% a month, written into the contract before work starts. Check your state’s limits; without an agreed rate, most states apply a statutory legal rate.

Can I add a late fee that was not in my contract?

It is much harder to enforce. Without an agreed rate, you can usually claim only your state’s legal interest rate. Add the late fee to your contract or quote for future clients.
