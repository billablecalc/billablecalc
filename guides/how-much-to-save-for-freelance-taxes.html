How Much Should Freelancers Save for Taxes?

When you are an employee, tax is taken from your pay before you see it. When you are a freelancer, clients pay you in full and the tax bill arrives later. Many new freelancers spend money that belongs to the tax authority. The fix is simple: decide a percentage and move it out of reach every time you are paid.

This guide gives a practical way to estimate that percentage. It is general information, not tax advice, and rules differ a lot between countries.

Start with profit, not income

Tax is based on your profit: income minus allowed business expenses. If you earn 80,000 and have 20,000 of deductible expenses, your taxable profit is 60,000, not 80,000. Keep records of every expense and receipt, because they directly lower your bill.

A simple rule of thumb

Many freelancers set aside about 25 to 30 percent of each payment. This is only a starting point. Your real rate depends on your country, your total income, deductions and other earnings. A freelancer with low profit may need less, and a high earner may need more.

Example: saving for the year

Profit of 60,000 and a combined tax rate of 28% gives a yearly tax of 16,800. That is 4,200 per quarter, or 1,400 per month. If you save 28% of every payment into a separate account, you will have the money when the bill comes.

If you are in the United States

US freelancers usually pay two things: income tax and self-employment tax. Self-employment tax covers Social Security and Medicare and is roughly 15.3% of 92.35% of net profit. On 60,000 profit that is about 8,478, and about half of it is deductible when calculating income tax. The Social Security part has an income cap, which simple estimates ignore.

Most freelancers there pay estimated tax in installments, roughly in mid-April, mid-June, mid-September and mid-January. A commonly used safe-harbor guideline is to pay at least 90% of the current year's tax or 100% of last year's, with a higher percentage for high earners. Check the current rules with the tax authority or an accountant.

If you are outside the United States

Systems differ. Some countries have flat taxes for the self-employed, some tax progressively, and some require VAT registration above a threshold. Social insurance is often separate. Look up your national rules or ask a local accountant, then replace the percentage in the example with your own.

Habits that make tax time easy

Open a separate savings account just for tax and transfer your percentage every time a client pays.

Record every expense with a receipt, a date and a short reason.

Keep business and personal money separate.

Review your numbers every quarter, and adjust the percentage if your income changes.

Track invoices so you always know what you are owed.

Common deductions

Typical deductible costs for freelancers include software, equipment, a share of home office costs, professional fees, training related to your work and business travel. What qualifies, and how much, depends on local law and on whether the cost is genuinely for the business.

Mistakes to avoid

Treating gross payments as your own money.

Mixing personal and business spending.

Ignoring tax until the deadline.

Assuming the rules are the same as when you were an employee.
