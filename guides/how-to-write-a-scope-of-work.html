How to Write a Scope of Work (With Example)

Most freelance disputes start with a sentence like "I thought that was included." A scope of work is a short document that removes that sentence from your career. It describes what you will deliver, how it will be accepted and what happens to requests that fall outside the plan.

What a scope of work contains

A good scope has six parts. You can fit them on one page.

1. Project summary

One or two sentences: what the project is and why the client wants it.

2. In scope

A list of the specific deliverables. Be concrete: "a five-page website with a contact form" is better than "a website". Include quantities, formats and any quality standard, such as the file types you will deliver.

3. Out of scope

Write down what is not included. Typical examples are hosting, content writing, photography, extra pages, ongoing maintenance and additional revision rounds. This list often prevents the most arguments, because it names the things clients tend to assume.

4. Client responsibilities

Say what you need from the client and by when: materials, access, approvals and feedback. For example, feedback within three business days. If the client is late, the timeline moves, and having it in writing makes that conversation easy.

5. Milestones and timeline

Break the project into stages with dates. Milestones let you invoice in parts and help the client see progress.

6. Change requests and acceptance

Explain that work outside the scope will be quoted separately and starts only after written approval. Define when the work counts as accepted, for example when the client approves in writing or when seven days pass after delivery without feedback.

A short example

Project: product landing page. In scope: one landing page, up to six sections, mobile layout, one contact form, two rounds of revisions. Out of scope: logo design, copywriting, hosting, additional pages. Client provides: text and images by the start date, feedback within three business days. Timeline: first draft in two weeks, final version in three weeks. Change requests: quoted separately in writing.

Tips

Use plain language. Number or bullet the items so they are easy to refer to. Attach the scope to your contract so it is part of the agreement. When a client asks for something new, point to the scope politely and send a quick quote. That is not being difficult, it is how professional projects work.
