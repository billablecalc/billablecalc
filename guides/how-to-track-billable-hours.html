How to Track Billable Hours Without Forgetting the Timer

Short answer: use a one-click timer with an hourly rate per client, turn on reminders so the timer starts when you open a client’s tools and asks about time you were away, then review your hours every Friday and invoice straight from them. Most lost hours come from two mistakes: a timer you forgot to start, and a timer you forgot to stop.

Where freelancers lose billable hours

  • The forgotten start. You open the client’s Figma file “just to check one thing” and look up 90 minutes later. That time is real work, but there is no record of it.
  • The forgotten stop. The timer runs through lunch or overnight. Now you have to guess what to cut, and guessing usually means billing less.
  • Switching clients. You jump from one client’s email to another’s Slack, and the timer keeps running for the wrong one.
  • Small tasks. Five-minute replies, calls and quick fixes add up to hours a week and rarely get tracked.
  • Rebuilding later. Filling in timesheets from memory on Friday is slow and you will always undercount.

Losing just 30 minutes a day at €50/hour is about €500 a month.

A simple system that catches every hour

1. One client, one rate

Set up each client once with their hourly rate and currency. When the amount grows in real time while you work, it is much harder to forget the timer.

2. Start the timer where the work starts

The most reliable trigger is the client’s own tools. In BillableCalc Timer you add each client’s sites (for example figma.com or acme.atlassian.net). When you open one with the timer off, you get a reminder: “Working for Acme? Start timer.”

3. Let the tracker ask about idle time

If you step away while the timer runs, a good tracker asks when you come back: keep that time or remove it? You decide in a second, while you still remember, instead of guessing at the end of the week.

4. Catch the forgotten stop

Turn on a “still working?” check after a few hours. It costs nothing on long focus days and saves you from a timer that ran all night.

5. Write one line per entry

“Homepage redesign” or “Client feedback call” is enough. These notes become the lines on your invoice and answer the client’s “what was this?” before they ask.

6. Review every Friday, invoice from the hours

Spend ten minutes on Friday: fix entries, add anything you missed, then create the invoice directly from the week’s hours. See how to write an invoice for what to include.

FAQ

What is the best way to track billable hours as a freelancer?

A one-click timer with a rate per client, reminders to start and stop, a weekly review and invoicing straight from your hours. Pick a tool simple enough that you actually use it every day.

How do I avoid forgetting to start my time tracker?

Tie it to the tools you use for each client. BillableCalc Timer reminds you to start when you open a client’s site with the timer off, and shows an orange “!” on its icon if you miss the notification.

Should I bill for time I was away from the computer?

Only if you were still working, for example on a call or sketching on paper. Otherwise remove it. Idle detection asks you right when you come back, so you decide while you still remember.

How many hours can a freelancer actually bill?

Usually 50–70% of working time. See billable hours and utilization rate.
