Late Payment Email Templates for Freelancers (Copy and Paste)

Chasing money is awkward, and that is exactly why a ready-made email helps. Below are three templates that move from friendly to firm. Replace the details in brackets and send them in order, only escalating if there is no response. Keep every message polite and in writing.

Before you send anything

Check that the invoice was actually delivered, that the amount and due date are right and that you are writing to the person who pays invoices. Many "late" payments are an invoice stuck in someone's inbox. If your contract has a late fee, mention it only from the second email onward.

Template 1: friendly reminder (1 to 3 days late)

Subject: Invoice [NUMBER] - quick reminder

Hi [NAME], just a quick reminder that invoice [NUMBER] for [AMOUNT] was due on [DATE]. If it has already been paid, thank you and please ignore this message. Otherwise, could you let me know when I can expect payment? The invoice is attached again for convenience. Thanks!

Why it works: it assumes an honest mistake, makes it easy to reply and gives the client a graceful way to pay.

Template 2: firm follow-up (7 to 14 days late)

Subject: Overdue: Invoice [NUMBER]

Hi [NAME], invoice [NUMBER] for [AMOUNT] is now [X] days overdue. Please arrange payment by [DATE]. If there is a problem with the invoice or the payment process, tell me and I will fix it right away. I would like to keep the project on schedule.

Why it works: it states the facts, sets a new date and offers help, which removes excuses.

Template 3: final notice (30 or more days late)

Subject: Final notice: Invoice [NUMBER]

Hi [NAME], I have not received payment for invoice [NUMBER] ([AMOUNT]), now [X] days overdue. Under our agreement, late interest of [1.5]% per month applies, which brings the total to [NEW TOTAL]. Please pay by [DATE]. If I do not hear from you by then, I will pause all work and consider collection options.

Why it works: it is calm, specific and states the consequence without threats.

How much interest to add

Interest is usually the invoice amount times the monthly rate times the days overdue divided by 30. For a 1,000 invoice at 1.5% per month that is 45 days late, the fee is 1,000 x 0.015 x 45 / 30 = 22.50. Our late fee calculator does the math for you. Only charge interest that your contract allows, and check the rules in your country.

If there is still no payment

Pause work if you are in the middle of a project and your contract allows it. Consider a formal letter, a collection service or small claims court depending on the amount and where you live. For small invoices, many freelancers decide the time is worth more than the money, but this is your call.

Prevent the next one

Ask for a deposit, state payment terms and the late fee in your contract, send invoices as soon as work is delivered and give clients an easy way to pay.
