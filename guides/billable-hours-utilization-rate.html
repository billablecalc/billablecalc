Billable Hours and Utilization Rate: How Many Hours Can You Really Bill?

When you set your freelance rate, one number matters more than almost any other: how many hours a year you can actually bill. It is common to overestimate it by a lot, and that mistake quietly lowers your income.

What billable hours are

Billable hours are the hours you can charge a client for. Time spent finding clients, writing proposals, sending invoices, answering general questions, learning new skills and managing your business is real work but not billable.

Utilization rate

Utilization rate is billable hours divided by total working hours. If you work 40 hours and bill 25, your utilization rate is 25 / 40 = 62.5%. Many freelancers land between 50 and 70 percent, though it varies by field and by how much work comes from repeat clients.

A worked example

Suppose you work 46 weeks a year at 40 hours, which is 1,840 hours. With a 60 percent utilization rate you bill 1,840 x 0.6 = 1,104 hours. If you need 80,000 in revenue, your rate must be 80,000 / 1,104 = about 72.46 per hour. If you wrongly assumed 1,840 billable hours, you would quote 43.48, and end up earning far less than your goal.

How to measure your own

Track your time for a month, even the non-billable parts. A simple spreadsheet or a free timer app is enough. Group the time into billable work, sales and marketing, admin and learning. After a month you will have a realistic percentage you can use in your pricing.

How to improve it

Reduce admin with templates for proposals, contracts and invoices.

Batch emails and calls instead of answering all day.

Keep long-term clients with retainers so you spend less time looking for work.

Say no to projects that cost more time in sales than they pay.

Raise rates, so you need fewer billable hours to reach your goal.

Do not chase 100 percent

A very high utilization rate usually means no time for marketing, rest or learning, which hurts your future income. A sustainable number leaves room for finding the next project.
