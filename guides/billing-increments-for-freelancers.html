Billing Increments for Freelancers: 6, 15 or 30 Minutes?

Short answer: pick one billing increment, put it in your contract, and round each time entry up to it. Most freelancers use 15 minutes. Consultants and lawyers often use 6 minutes (a tenth of an hour). The point is not to overcharge: it is to stop giving away the dozens of 4-minute tasks that never make it onto an invoice.

What a billing increment is

A billing increment is the smallest unit of time you bill. With a 15-minute increment, a 4-minute email is billed as 15 minutes and a 20-minute call as 30 minutes. Each task is rounded up to the next increment.

  • 6 minutes (0.1 h): 1–6 min = 0.1 h, 7–12 min = 0.2 h. Precise, and easy to read as decimals on an invoice.
  • 15 minutes (0.25 h): the common freelance default. Simple for clients to understand.
  • 30 minutes (0.5 h): only makes sense when most of your work comes in long blocks.

Example: one day of small tasks

Say you bill €60/hour and one day looks like this:

TaskActual6-min15-min30-min
Reply to client email4 min61530
Fix a typo on the site9 min121530
Feedback call22 min243030
Design revisions95 min96105120
Total130 min138 min165 min210 min
Billed at €60/h€130.00€138.00€165.00€210.00

The 6-minute increment adds about 6% here, the 15-minute one about 27%. That difference is not padding: it covers the real cost of switching context for a 4-minute task, which never takes only 4 minutes of your attention.

Round each entry, not the weekly total

Rounding a weekly total of 13 h 7 min to 13 h 15 min changes almost nothing. The increment only works when it is applied to each task, because that is where the small, interrupted work lives. Keep the actual minutes in your own records so you can always show the client how a number was reached.

Put the rule in your contract

Rounding is fair when the client knows it in advance. One sentence is enough:

“Time is billed in 15-minute increments per task, rounded up, at €60 per hour.”

Add it to the payment section of your contract or proposal. See how to write a freelance contract and sample clauses.

FAQ

Should freelancers bill for 5-minute tasks?

Yes, if they are work for the client: replies, quick fixes and short calls. Agree on a billing increment in your contract, such as 15 minutes, so small tasks are billed consistently instead of forgotten.

What is a 6-minute billing increment?

Time is billed in tenths of an hour: 1 to 6 minutes is 0.1 h, 7 to 12 minutes is 0.2 h, and so on. It is common in law and consulting because it is fair to the client and easy to read on an invoice.

Is rounding time up ethical?

Yes, when the client knows the rule in advance. Put the increment in your contract or proposal, apply it to every entry, and show the actual minutes in your own records.

Should I round each entry or the weekly total?

Round each entry. Rounding only the weekly total gives almost the same result as no rounding, while rounding each task is what pays for the switching cost of small tasks.

What increment should I choose?

15 minutes is the most common choice for freelancers. Use 6 minutes if your clients are used to legal or consulting invoices, and 30 minutes only if most of your tasks are long.
